Subtopic 3: Smart Irrigation and Automated Water Management

URL https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/smart-irrigation-water-management-a-cornerstone-of-sustainable-ipm-in-vegetable-production/
Site Author Paulo Gimenez Cremonez and Andre da Silva, Alabama Cooperative Extension System
Abstract This Alabama Cooperative Extension publication explains how smart irrigation technologies improve water conservation and integrated pest management in vegetable crop production. The authors compare traditional flood and overhead sprinkler irrigation with modern drip and subsurface drip systems, noting that drip irrigation reaches eighty-five to ninety-five percent water efficiency by delivering moisture directly to plant root zones. Keeping crop foliage dry minimizes fungal diseases and reduces favorable habitats for agricultural pests. Furthermore, integrating automated timers with in-ground soil moisture sensors prevents overwatering, lowers energy and water costs, and stops nutrient leaching. The guide also outlines practical strategies for matching irrigation equipment to local soil textures and field topography, showing how automated water delivery strengthens both crop resilience and environmental sustainability.

Site Author Esther N. Lofton, University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources (UC ANR)
Abstract This article from University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources details how smart irrigation controllers conserve freshwater and improve plant health. Unlike conventional timers that water on rigid schedules regardless of weather, smart controllers automatically adjust watering cycles using real-time meteorological feeds, evapotranspiration calculations, and underground soil moisture sensors. According to cited research, these automated systems can cut outdoor water consumption by twenty to fifty percent and increase irrigation efficiency by thirty percent. The article highlights Wi-Fi-enabled controllers that allow growers and land managers to monitor schedules remotely via smartphone applications. By delivering precise moisture amounts only when plants require hydration, smart irrigation technology lowers utility expenses, prevents soil erosion, protects local waterways from chemical runoff, and supports healthier yields in drought-prone regions.
