Subtopic 2: Vertical Farming and Controlled Environment Agriculture

URL https://www.ars.usda.gov/oc/utm/vertical-farming-no-longer-a-futuristic-concept/
Site Author Georgia Jiang, USDA Agricultural Research Service (ARS)
Abstract This USDA Agricultural Research Service interview with plant pathologist Dr. Kai-Shu Ling and horticulturalist Dr. James Altland explores how vertical farming is transitioning into a practical agricultural method. Unlike conventional open-field farming, vertical farming stacks crops indoors under controlled lighting, temperature, and nutrient-rich hydroponic solutions without soil. The researchers explain that enclosed environments shield plants from outdoor pests and foodborne pathogens like E. coli, reducing or eliminating the need for chemical pesticides while delivering fresh, locally grown produce year-round. While leafy greens, herbs, and small fruiting crops such as tomatoes and cucumbers thrive indoors, staple row crops like corn and wheat remain better suited for traditional farmland. The article also notes ongoing USDA collaborations with NASA to adapt controlled environment agriculture for long-duration space missions.
URL https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1848227/full
Site Author Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems Research Authors
Abstract This peer-reviewed study in Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems investigates how commercial vertical farming companies adopt advanced technologies from both technical and business strategy perspectives. Using the Technology-Organization-Environment framework, the authors evaluate key innovations including energy-efficient LED lighting, automated robotics, environmental sensors, closed-loop water filtration, and artificial intelligence platforms. Machine learning algorithms continuously analyze sensor data to optimize plant nutrient recipes and adjust climate equipment, lowering electricity expenses while maximizing crop yield and quality. Beyond engineering capabilities, the paper emphasizes that financial capital, operational scalability, and market competition heavily influence which technologies companies implement. The study demonstrates that pairing automated indoor hardware with smart data analytics is essential for making urban vertical farms economically sustainable and competitive with conventional agriculture.
