Subtopic 1: Precision Agriculture and Smart Sensors

URL https://www.nsf.gov/science-matters/advancing-farming-cutting-edge-technologies
Site Author U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF)
Abstract This article from the U.S. National Science Foundation examines how precision agriculture technologies are modernizing crop production while lowering costs and conserving vital resources. It highlights research initiatives that deploy in-field sensors, aerial drones, and autonomous ground robots to gather real-time data on plant nitrogen levels, soil conditions, and crop health. By using artificial intelligence and predictive digital twins, farmers can detect early signs of plant disease and tailor fertilizer or water delivery to specific areas rather than treating entire fields uniformly. The article also discusses efforts to expand rural broadband infrastructure and mitigate GPS interference so growers can reliably operate connected farm equipment. Overall, the source demonstrates how federally supported engineering and data analytics help farmers overcome labor shortages, increase crop yields, reduce chemical waste, and strengthen national food security.
URL https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105962
Site Author U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO)
Abstract This report by the U.S. Government Accountability Office evaluates the economic benefits, environmental impacts, and adoption barriers surrounding precision agriculture technologies in the United States. The source describes several emerging tools, including in-ground soil moisture and nutrient sensors, remote sensing drones, machine-learning-guided targeted sprayers, and automated mechanical weeders that minimize crop damage. According to the report, these systems enable farmers to boost profitability by maintaining or increasing crop yields while significantly reducing fertilizer, herbicide, fuel, and water inputs. Reduced chemical application also protects nearby waterways from nutrient runoff. However, the GAO identifies major obstacles preventing wider adoption, such as high upfront equipment expenses, lack of technical interoperability standards across brands, and farmer concerns regarding agricultural data privacy and ownership. The report concludes with policy options aimed at improving data standards and analytical support for growers.
